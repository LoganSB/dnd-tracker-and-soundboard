# DM Soundboard

    python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
    .venv/bin/python app.py            # http://127.0.0.1:5000
    .venv/bin/python app.py --live     # ignore the launch campaign, load live changes
    .venv/bin/python app.py --campaign NAME

Sounds are downloaded once into `media/` (yt-dlp for YouTube, scraped mp3 for MyInstants), then
decoded in the browser so playback is instant. State lives in `data/` (live.json, written when the pages save: every 30 seconds after a change, or at once with Ctrl+S,
versions/ snapshot per server start, presets/ = your saved campaigns, soundboards/ = your saved soundboards, config.json launch choice, users.json accounts).

## Accounts: DM and players
`/` is a banner page with the app's header and a **Login** button. There are two permission levels, enforced by the server on every request (`auth.py`):
- **DM** sees everything: Home, Soundboard, Campaign, Characters, the party tracker, accounts and invites.
- **Player** sees only Home and **My character**: they can upload a Fight Club file (the same import as before) and view or edit what they uploaded. Everyone else's characters, and every other page, answer "forbidden".

The DM's **Home** tab lists the saved campaigns, the party, every uploaded character with the player who owns it (changeable), the accounts, and invite links. Players cannot register on their own: the DM creates a single-use invite link (valid 7 days). The player opens it, types a user name and presses **Next**, then either sets a password (typed twice) or presses the standard **Sign up with Google** button.
- **Linking logins (done by the person, not the DM).** Someone who signed up with Google and also has a user name and password here opens their Home tab and uses the small "Link this login" line in the footer (a pop-up): they type that user name and its password. The Google login is folded into that account (characters, campaign memberships and the Google address move across) and the browser is signed in as it. From then on either way of signing in opens the same account and data. A DM can still unlink a Google address from the Players list; the DM cannot link accounts.
- **Password resets** are offered only for accounts that have a password. A Google-only account has nothing to reset (the person's Google account handles that), and setting one would quietly add a second way in that the DM chose.
- **Campaigns and join requests.** Players see the campaign names (nothing inside them) and press **Request to join**; the request appears at the top of the DM's Home tab to approve or deny. Each account records the campaigns it belongs to; a player whose character is added to a campaign's party joins that campaign automatically.
Sign-in is by user name and password (stored only as salted hashes in `data/users.json`) and optionally Google. **First run:** a local copy with no accounts stays open (everyone is the DM) until you create an account on `/login`; a deployed copy (which has `DND_PASSWORD` set) is locked, and `/login` offers "Create the DM account", which asks for `DND_PASSWORD` as the setup key. Google sign-in needs `DND_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `DND_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` and (optionally) `DND_DM_EMAIL`; see the top of `auth.py`.

## Campaigns
**Campaign** is the new name for what used to be called a preset: a named save of the campaign tracker, the combat and the party (stored in `data/presets/`). **Soundboards are separate files** (`data/soundboards/`: sections, sounds, timers) and a campaign points at two of them: its **default** soundboard and the **last** one used with it. Loading a campaign from Home loads its tracker, party and combat AND its default soundboard (Home looks the same as before); starting the server with a launch campaign brings its last-used soundboard. On the **Campaign tab** a drop-down loads another campaign's tracker, party and combat only (the soundboard on screen stays), **Default soundboard** chooses which soundboard Home brings in, and **Save campaign** writes the campaign to its file. On the **Soundboard tab** a drop-down loads another soundboard (the campaign tracker is untouched), **Clone** copies the soundboard on screen under a new name and switches to the copy, and **Soundboards & versions** lists, loads, deletes (not while one is on screen or a campaign uses it) and creates empty ones. The soundboard on screen autosaves into its own file; a campaign is written when you press Save. Campaign files from before the split are converted when the server starts: each becomes a soundboard (named after the campaign) plus a campaign pointing at it, and the original is copied into `data/versions/`. It also carries the list of **party members**, saved as references only (character id, name, owner), never the character sheets. Each sheet stays with the player who uploaded it (`data/party/<id>.xml`), so loading a campaign brings back who is in the party, and each member appears as soon as their file is on the server (until then Home says "waiting for <player>"). On the Home tab, the **Characters** list holds every uploaded character that is not in the campaign; **Add to campaign** moves one into **Party in <campaign>** (and out of the Characters list), and **Remove from campaign** moves it back. Because the party list is part of the campaign file, loading another campaign swaps the lists automatically. The party tracker panel shows just the party.

## Encounters, initiative and the canvas menu
- **Encounters tab (DM).** Find monsters from [Open5e](https://open5e.com) (free; includes the Kobold Press books such as Tome of Beasts): all of them are listed A to Z, and typing narrows the list live with suggestions that show each CR; partial words, single-letter typos, regular expressions and the CR ("cr 5", "cr 1/2", "3-5", "5+") all work, plus filters for type, book and CR. To keep Open5e calls to a minimum, the whole monster list is fetched once (2 calls), saved in `data/monsters_index.json` and searched in the browser (rebuilt after 30 days, or by hand at most daily); each stat block is fetched once and saved in `data/monsters/`. Add them to an encounter, and see its difficulty for the party (DMG thresholds; party size and level come from the campaign's party and can be changed). Encounters and loot tables belong to no campaign: they are a shared library in `data/encounters.json` and `data/loots.json`, usable from every campaign, and each record notes which saved campaigns have it on their tracker (the Browse lists filter on that). Older saves that kept them inside the campaign are moved into the library automatically. Buttons: load into the initiative tracker (new combat or add to the current one), load and pop out the tracker window, and put the encounter on the campaign tracker as a card (the view then switches to the campaign tracker and highlights the new card, the same jump the 🎯 buttons use for sounds).
- **Spells list.** Like the monster and item lists, the spell list (about 1,400 spells from the SRD and the Kobold Press / Deep Magic books) is downloaded from Open5e once into `data/spells_index.json` (rebuilt at most monthly) and searched in the browser (`static/spells.js`); each spell's full text is fetched once into `data/spells/`.
- **Cables.** When one card has several cables leaving it each has its own pattern (solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, long dash; a sixth repeats the first); a cable whose source is not done yet is dimmer instead of dashed (`Cables.patterns` in `static/cables.js`).
- **Encounter maps.** An encounter can hold up to 20 maps (png, jpg, gif, webp or pdf, 25 MB each): upload, view in a new tab, download and delete them under the editor on the Encounters tab, or from the 🗺 button on an encounter card on the campaign tracker (the same panel, `static/maps.js`). They are stored in `data/encounter_maps/<encounter id>/` and removed with the encounter; the upload code is shared with the sound library (`uploads.py`).
- **Rests.** The character card has **Short rest** and **Long rest** buttons inline with the name (they replaced the separate long-rest buttons). Short rest (5e): choose how many hit dice to spend; each is rolled with your Constitution modifier and heals that much (never above your maximum); a Warlock's Pact Magic slots return. Long rest: all hit points, spent hit dice back up to half your total (at least 1), all spell slots, exhaustion down by 1, death saves cleared, and your own trackers (Mana, Ki, Rage ...) back to full; it asks first. What happened is shown under the name. Class features that recharge on a short rest are not tracked unless you made a tracker for them.
- **Conditions change the stats.** A condition on a character shows its 5e description and the lines it affects. The Statistics tiles then apply it and say why: speed becomes 0 (Grappled, Restrained, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned, Unconscious, Exhaustion 5) or half (Prone, Exhaustion 2-4), passive Perception drops by 5 for conditions that give disadvantage on ability checks (Poisoned, Frightened, Exhaustion 1+). A changed tile is outlined, shows the old number struck through and names the condition; disadvantage on attack rolls and saves, no actions and a halved hit point maximum are shown as warnings. Exhaustion has levels (arrows on its chip). All of the rules are one table, `static/conditions.js`; a custom condition has no description or effect.
- **Ability scores.** The score the app shows is what Fight Club shows: the BASE score from the file plus every score bonus (`<mod category=1>`): the race, each level-up's Ability Score Improvement (they are stored on the class), feats and active items: armor, shields and weapons when equipped, anything else (a belt, a ring, a cloak) when it is in the pack's main list rather than stowed away. (Lynius: Wisdom 17 + 1 race + 2 level-ups = 20; Strength 11 + 3 + his Belt of Stone Giant Strength 9 = 23, as on the phone.) The tooltip on a score says where the bonus comes from. An active item's armor class bonus counts the same way.
- **Edit character.** The **✎ Edit character** button opens a pop-up for the name, XP, hit point maximum, base ability scores (it shows the resulting score as you type), race and looks (speed blank = 30), background, class name / level / hit die / armor / weapons / tools, the maximum spell slots, and the saving throw and skill proficiencies (`static/editchar.js`; written into the Fight Club file by `fcedit.py`).
- **Giving items.** **Give** moves any number of an item to another player's character (a DM can give to anyone; a player to the other players' characters in the loaded campaign's party). It lands among the receiver's loose items, highlighted with "Given to you by <player>" and an **Acknowledge** button; the highlight stays until it is acknowledged (kept beside the file, not in it).
- **Notes boxes.** The notes on an encounter or loot table grow to fit their text (at least 2 lines, up to 10 lines tall, then they scroll) and hold at most 100 lines / 8,000 characters.
- **Loot tab (DM).** The Encounters tab's twin: the same editor (`static/builder.js`) and the same search (`static/finder.js`), with Open5e magic items in place of monsters. Build loot tables (items with weights; the table shows its die and each item's chance and has a Roll button) and put one on the campaign tracker as a flag that rolls on it. The item list is fetched once (1 call) into `data/magicitems_index.json`, and each item's text once into `data/magicitems/`. In the campaign tracker a flag's 🎁 pop-up now has **Rewards from**: its own list, a loot table (with **New loot table…** / **Edit table…**, which open the Loot tab's editor in a small form), or a random magic item of a chosen rarity.
- **Browse lists.** The bottom of the Home tab has **Browse encounters** and **Browse loot tables**; the Encounters tab has the first and the Loot tab the second. They search what is *inside* each record (type `goblin` or `boots`) and have an Open button (`static/browse.js`). Every searchable table keeps its regular-expression search (the whole text as one expression over the row, as before) and now also finds partial words and single-letter typos, using the matching the monster and item searches share (`static/finder.js`); regex matches are listed first.
- **Never doubled:** adding an encounter that is already in the combat does nothing (the card says "Already in the tracker"; the builder asks before adding another set). Starting a new combat always works.
- **Errors appear next to the button.** `static/errors.js` (loaded on every page) shows a failed button's message in red right after that button instead of replacing the page or opening a pop-up. Downloads are buttons that check the answer first (`safeDownload`).
- **Encounter cards** on the campaign canvas point at the same encounter: clicking one adds it to the initiative tracker; its buttons start a new combat, load and pop out, or open it in the Encounters tab. Making a card with "New encounter" from the canvas creates the encounter too, and opens the Encounters tab on it.
- **Initiative is per campaign** (`state["initiative"]`) and the tracker only lists the loaded campaign's party; characters who leave the party leave the combat. A monster's book button opens its stat block (`/monster/<slug>`).
- **Cables.** Chains on the soundboard and links on the campaign tracker are drawn by the same code (`static/cables.js`): all cables are routed together around the cards, so cables that share a road run side by side as lanes instead of stacking, and the cable you are dragging follows the pointer even when it goes behind where it started.
- **Lists.** Every list (the monster, item and spell searches, and all tables such as Browse, Players and the character sheet's lists) shows 20 rows at a time and has PAGES: up to four buttons at the bottom left (**10 / 20 / 50 / 100**) set the size (the chosen one is highlighted; clicking it again does nothing) and arrows « ‹ › » at the right move between pages, with a counter such as "21-40 of 74" (or "1-2 of 2 (filtered from 74)" when a search or filter hides rows). A size button only appears when the list is longer than the size before it: 10 rows show none, 11 rows show 10 and 20, 30 rows show 10, 20 and 50, 51 or more show all four. The size is one choice shared by all lists, remembered in the browser; all of it is one shared component (`Finder.pageSize.pager` in `static/finder.js`), so a new list gets it for free.  The search pop-ups (Add loot, Add spell, Add companion) use the same pager. A table's own search box and filter menus only appear when the list is longer than one page of the selected size (a short list is just read; lower the size and they come back); the big searches (monsters, items, spells) always have theirs. Editing an encounter or loot table anywhere refreshes the Browse lists on Home and the other tabs at once.
- **Click menu.** Click empty canvas for a small menu, on the campaign tracker (new section, flag, encounter, loot table, condition or timer) and on the soundboard (new section, sound or timer), created where you clicked and inside the innermost section you clicked in. A section's **+** button opens the same menu for that section. All three places share one list, `createEntries` on each page. Both pages share one implementation, `Canvas.clickMenu` in `static/canvas.js`.
- **Home lists** are all collapsible (click the heading), searchable with a regular expression and filterable per column; the Party and Characters lists are one table with the same columns.

## Party
- `/party` import (.backup/.xml/.zip from Fight Club 5), export (zip with FightClub5.backup), per-character full pages at `/party/<id>`.
- Tracker: the **Party** button on the soundboard (floating panel) or `/tracker` (pop-out window).
- **Importing asks which characters.** Choosing a backup (Home's upload box or the Characters page) first looks inside it (`POST /api/party/preview`, nothing saved) and shows a pop-up with a checkbox per character (already-present ones are marked "will be updated"; a player cannot tick one that belongs to someone else). Each ticked character is then saved on its own (`POST /api/party/import` with `select` and one `only` id each), so one failing does not stop the rest. Shared code: `static/importer.js`.
- **Exports are Fight Club's own file format**: one plain XML `.backup` (not a zip), written by `fcformat.py` so that the six real characters come out byte for byte as Fight Club wrote them, portraits included (they are now kept on import). The rules are in `FIGHTCLUB-FORMAT.md`; `?format=zip` still gives the old zipped form.
- **Character sheet (the Characters tab).** The tab always shows ONE character's sheet (the one opened from Home, else the last one used, else the party's first); a picker beside the download button switches character. Home lists and uploads characters (there is no separate character list any more). **Download all characters** (in the Characters tab's toolbar and on Home) saves every character in ONE Fight Club backup file you can import into the app; a player's button holds only the characters assigned to them. The sheet has five sub-menu pages: **Tracker** (one card: the live tracker for that character, then Hit dice, and under thin lines Conditions (chips with an **+ Add** menu; under them what each condition does, from the 5e rules, and what it changes on your stats), Statistics (AC, initiative, speed (30 ft when the race does not say; +10 ft Fast Movement for a Barbarian), passive Perception, proficiency, inspiration), Attacks (equipped weapons with attack bonus and damage plus type) and Creatures (the character's Fight Club companions, with hit points and AC read from and written to the file, so the phone app sees them; click a row for the whole stat block (traits, actions, saves, skills ...), **✎** edits hit points and AC, and **+ Add companion** searches the monster list and writes the companion in Fight Club's format, which the phone app imports). The class's own counters in the file (Channel Divinity ...) show with the personal trackers, and Short rest refills the ones marked as returning on a short rest, **Skills and Stats** (abilities and saving throws, every skill), **Inventory** (change the count of a stack with − / +, the last one removes it; **Equip / Unequip** anything wearable, with the limits below; **Attune** for items that need it; **Give** an item to another player; ✕ removes it; **+ Add loot** searches the magic items and **+ Add item** makes any item by hand), **Spells** (with the spell slot tracker above the list) and **Feats, Features and Notes**. Every list is the same searchable table as the rest of the app; click a row to open its description. Each of the last three has an Add button: **+ Add loot** and **+ Add spell** (it starts filtered to the character's class; choose Any class to see all) open a search pop-up on the Open5e magic item and spell lists (the same searches the Loot tab and the encounter builder use), and **+ Add note** opens a small form; what you add is written into the character's Fight Club file, so it is in the next export. AC, initiative, passive Perception, proficiency, attack bonuses and skill modifiers are worked out from the character's ability scores, level, equipped items and proficiencies (`charcalc.py`); bonuses from class features and spells are not included. Class features come from the character's own feature list (Fight Club's, so a druid shows only their own circle).
- Characters are stored as their original Fight Club XML in `data/party/`; HP, slots, hit dice, inspiration and money edits are written back so export round-trips. "Mana"/conditions have no Fight Club field, so they are kept in `data/party_extra.json` and are not exported.

## Wearing items, attunement and the limits

Equip enforces what a character can wear at once, and the message names the item in the way. Real 5e rules (DMG, "Wearing and Wielding Items"; "Attunement"): one suit of armor, one pair of boots, one pair of gloves or gauntlets, one pair of bracers, one item of headwear, one cloak, and attunement to at most **3** magic items (the **Attune** button, only for items whose text says they need it; their bonuses count only while attuned). House rules this app adds because the book has no limit: **1 belt, 1 amulet, 10 rings, 2 bags, two hands** (a two-handed weapon takes both, a shield cannot be taken up beside one). The kind an item is worn as comes from its name ("Belt of Giant Strength", "Winged Boots", "Ring of ..."); only an item whose name and description say nothing asks where it is worn. New items arrive stowed and count for nothing until equipped. Armor, shields and weapons use Fight Club's own slots; every other worn kind and the attunement live in the extras (`gear.py`), not in the Fight Club file.

## Character files: Fight Club 5e and Gloan5e

The character download offers two formats (the choice is remembered): **Fight Club 5e**, the plain backup the Fight Club app reads, and **Gloan5e** (`.gloan5e`), the same backup plus this app's extras (worn items, attunement, conditions, trackers, death saves) in one zip, so nothing is lost moving between servers. The Fight Club download is the Gloan5e one with the extras stripped, so it is byte-for-byte what it always was. An upload is recognised by its file, not by asking; a Fight Club file is converted to the same record with no extras. Layout and rules: `GLOAN5E-FORMAT.md` and `gloan5e.py`.

## Icon
`static/icon.jpeg` is the browser tab icon (every page names it in a `<link rel="icon">`, and `/favicon.ico` serves it too) and the round image in front of "DND Tracker" in the banner (`templates/_nav.html`, `login.html`, `invite.html`, `landing.html`; styled by `.brandicon`). ## Conditions and triggers (campaign tracker)
A **condition** card (menu: New condition, or the **+ Condition** button) gathers the nodes linked into it and unlocks the nodes linked out of it once enough of them are done, for example "3 of 8 = marginal success, 5 of 8 = full success".
- **Groups and tiers.** A card has one or more *groups* (separate condition groupings in the same node); each group has *tiers*: a count and a name. A tier is reached when at least that many of the group's linked nodes are done ("done" is the usual meaning: a flag triggered, a checklist entry ticked, a card finished, or another condition card at its top tier). The card shows each group as `done/total` with a progress bar and its tiers lit as they are reached.
- **What each link is for.** Every link INTO the card counts toward one group, and every link OUT of it is unlocked by one group's tier. Choose these in the card's settings (click the card or ⚙); the settings open by themselves when you link into a card with several groups, or out of one with several tiers. The default is the first group, and for an output its highest tier.
- **Unlock highlight.** The moment an output becomes unlocked, its wire and its target card flash, the same flash used when a jump from the Encounters tab lands on a card. The wire turns solid green and the target stops being "locked". Opening the page flashes nothing; only changes do. Loops of conditions simply count as not met.
- Stored with the campaign: the card is an item with `condition = { groups: [{ id, name, tiers: [{ id, need, label }] }] }`; links get `group` (incoming) or `when = { group, tier }` (outgoing). The logic is `groupState`, `linkSatisfied` and `condDone` in `static/campaign.js`.

## Sound amp (for quiet recordings)
A sound's settings have an **Amp** slider from 1 (off) to 4 (about +12 dB), for recordings that are quiet even with the card's volume slider at full. The sound plays at its volume times its amp (`level()` in `static/app.js`), a boosted card shows a small `×2.0` badge, and changing the amp takes effect at once on a sound that is playing. Very loud sounds boosted a lot can distort.

## Your own audio files (mp3s/ folder)
Drop audio files (.mp3 .ogg .wav .m4a .opus .aac .flac) into `mp3s/`, or use **Browse...** in the add/edit sound dialog (it uploads the picked file into that folder). The server rescans the folder every 2 seconds, and the dialog's drop-down lists what is there. A sound using one is saved as `library:<file name>`; if the file goes missing the card shows "failed" and recovers by itself when the file comes back.

## Campaign tracker (/campaign)
Same layout as the soundboard (nested sections, drag and drop), but the clickable items are **flags** the party has triggered. A flag can have a checklist (the card grows to fit it, up to a per-flag max width), a loot table you can roll on (🎁), and an optional soundboard sound that plays when it is triggered (the soundboard page must be open). Campaign data is stored inside the same state file as the sounds, so every preset and version snapshot includes it.

## The canvas (pan and zoom, like ComfyUI)
The soundboard and the campaign tracker are infinite canvases. Drag empty space to pan (or hold Space and drag, or middle-mouse drag), use the mouse wheel to zoom (Shift + wheel pans sideways), and use the buttons in the bottom-right corner to zoom or **Fit** everything into view. Drag a card by the bar along its top to move it. Each section is drawn as a coloured frame around its cards and the sections inside it (drag the frame's title bar to move the whole section). The first card goes in the middle of the canvas and new cards are placed in the nearest free spot around their section. See `static/canvas.js`.

## One app, several pages at once
Once signed in, every address (`/`, `/sound`, `/campaign`, `/party`) serves the same shell page (`templates/shell.html`): one shared header ("DND Tracker", always linking to Home, with the current page highlighted) and the Home, Soundboard, Campaign and Characters pages loaded together as frames (a player gets only Home and Characters). The soundboard now lives at `/sound`; `/` is Home. Switching tabs only shows/hides a frame, so the soundboard keeps playing (and its timers keep running) while you look at the other pages. The real pages are served under `/_/...`; `/tracker` stays a stand-alone pop-out window.

## Campaign links
In the campaign tracker, drag from the circle on the right edge of ANY item (section, flag, checklist entry, a flag's loot table) onto any other item to link them. A cable is solid green once the item it comes from is done (flag triggered, entry ticked, loot rolled, card/section finished) and dashed otherwise; items still waiting on something are shown dimmed ("locked") with a tooltip. Click a cable to remove it. Flags have a 🗑 button.

## Switching focus between playing chains
When more than one chain is playing, one is "in focus" (the arrow keys, the chain buttons and the highlight act on it; the others are dimmed less than idle chains). A **"Chains playing" bar** appears at the bottom with one chip per playing chain: click a chip to move the focus there (the canvas pans to it if needed), or use `[` and `]`. The corner badge of a sound playing in a background chain also turns into a ◎ focus button. None of these touch the audio; clicking a playing sound itself still just stops it in one click. A background chain advancing by itself never steals the focus, and if the focused sound ends, focus falls back to another playing chain.


## Cards and frames (soundboard and campaign share the same layout code)
The movable things are the **cards**: sounds on the soundboard, flags on the campaign page. Each card sits at its own position (drag it by the bar along its top). Sections are **frames** drawn around the cards (and the sections) that belong to them and resized automatically. Drag a frame's title bar to move everything inside it. Things are **sticky**: a card or section stays in its section wherever you drag it (its frame just stretches to follow). The only ways to change section are to drop it on the **title bar (header) of another section**, or to press its **⤴ move up** button, which takes it out of its section into that section's parent (the only way to remove something from a section). While you carry something, the headers it may be dropped on are outlined in yellow, and the one under the pointer is solid. Dragging only goes sideways or deeper: something in a depth-2 section may be dropped on sections at depth 2 or deeper (top level is depth 0), never itself or something inside it; going shallower is what ⤴ is for. The section settings and sound dialogs offer the same choices.

**Snap** (button in the bottom-right corner, remembered): while dragging, cards snap to the grid and line up with other cards (left / centre / right and top / middle / bottom) with pink guide lines. Hold Alt to skip snapping for one drag.

## Links
Soundboard: many inputs, ONE output per sound. Campaign: any number of inputs AND outputs per item (each side shows one port per link plus an empty dashed one for the next link). On both pages you remove a link by clicking its output circle, its input square, or the cable itself; dragging a filled port re-routes it.

## Personal trackers on characters
The tracker has no built-in Mana bar. Use **+ Tracker** on a character's card to give THAT character a counter (Mana, Ki, Rage ...). Characters without one show nothing. They are stored beside the Fight Club data, not in the export. (An older single "Mana" value that was in use is kept as one tracker.)

The pop-out tracker window (Party > pop out) has no shared header: just the Party and Initiative tabs. On a phone (600px wide or less) the header is only the banner and the log-out button: the page links (and the Party tracker button) fold into a ☰ menu, and a ▤ button in the header hides or shows the toolbar at the top of the page you are on (the Characters tab's picker and download buttons, the boards' buttons) to give a phone screen more room; the choice is remembered.


## Snapping options, party button, jump-to-sound
- **Snap menu** (⚙ next to Snap in the bottom-right controls): turn the grid and card-to-card alignment on or off separately, and choose which cards count for alignment: every card, or only cards in the same section.
- **Party** now sits in the shared header next to the page links and works on every tab (the panel opens at the top right; "pop out" still opens the stand-alone window).
- On the campaign page the 🎯 button on a flag (and on checklist entries that have a sound) jumps to that sound: it switches to the Soundboard tab, centres the canvas on the card and flashes it. A checklist entry can be given a sound in the flag's edit dialog; ticking it plays the sound, like triggering a flag that has one.


## Undo / redo (everything on the soundboard and the campaign tracker)
Every change on both pages can be undone and redone: creating or deleting a section, sound, timer or card, renaming, moving things, editing in a dialog, volume and amp, ticking a checklist, collapsing a section, making, re-routing or removing a link. Use **Ctrl/Cmd+Z** to undo and **Ctrl/Cmd+Y** (or Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z) to redo, or the ↶ ↷ buttons in the toolbar; a message names what was undone. Each page keeps its own history for as long as it is open (up to 200 steps), and typing in a text box keeps the browser's normal text undo.

It is a **command stack** (`static/undo.js`): every change registers a command (a label plus an undo and a redo function) on a last-in-first-out stack, and undo walks the newest one back with one call. Instead of writing reverse code for each kind of change, a command's undo and redo restore the page's state from before and after the change. The page's `save()` reports every change to the stack (`edits.commit()`), so a new feature is undoable without extra code; `edits.record("deleted a section")` just gives the step a good name. Changes made a moment apart under the same name (typing, dragging a slider) merge into one step. Not covered: the encounter and loot libraries (their own files) and the combat in the initiative tracker.

## Objects stand on their own
Every object may have a parent but depends on none. **Deleting a section keeps everything inside it**: its sections, sounds, timers and cards move up to the section's own parent (or to the top level, where they have no section). Dropping one on a section's header gives it a parent again. Chains, links and playing sounds are unaffected. You can even delete every section.

## Sections nest
There are no tiles or subsections: a **section** is the one kind of container, and it can sit inside another section to any depth (drag its title bar into another section's frame, or use ⚙ and *Inside*). Each section has its own volume slider, fade-out and crossfade settings on the soundboard; volumes nest, so turning a section down also turns down every section inside it. Older saves with tiles and subsections are converted automatically when they load (each tile becomes a section inside its old section, each subsection a section inside its tile); the conversion is in `board.py`.

## Timers
A **timer** card exists on both the soundboard and the campaign tracker (toolbar *+ Timer*, the click menu, or a section's **+**). Set hours, minutes and seconds in its ⚙ settings; Start, Pause and Reset are on the card. It counts real clock time, so it keeps going while the page is closed and is finished the moment you open the page. When it finishes the card flashes for ten seconds and stays marked *Done*, the **Soundboard** or **Campaign** tab link in the top bar flashes until you open that tab, and the sound you picked in its settings (optional) plays: on the soundboard right away, from the campaign page through the soundboard (which must be open, as for a flag's linked sound). A timer that ran out while the page was closed flashes the tab but does not play its sound, which would be long after the fact. The shared code is `static/timers.js`; the settings dialog is the shared `Nodes.form` in `static/nodes.js`.

## Cables are coloured by where they start
On the campaign tracker each cable takes the colour of the section it starts in (solid = satisfied, dashed = waiting).

## Running it on Google Cloud
`Dockerfile`, `gunicorn.conf.py` and `.dockerignore` package the app as a container, and **DEPLOY-GOOGLE-CLOUD.md** has the step-by-step instructions to upload it to Cloud Run with a Cloud Storage bucket for your data and a password for the site. Settings read from the environment: `DND_DATA_ROOT` (where data/, media/ and mp3s/ live), `DND_PASSWORD` (the setup key for the first DM account; setting it locks the site until that account exists) and `PORT`, plus the optional Google sign-in settings above.
